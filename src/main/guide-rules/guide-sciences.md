
# Natural and Health Sciences

For biology, anatomy, physiology, chemistry and similar courses. These courses mostly ask students to explain how a process works, connect a structure to its function and location, use a lot of terminology correctly, and predict what happens when something changes. The guide is built from clear process explanations, labeled diagrams and structure-function tables, with retrieval and prediction practice in the week quizzes.

Follow guide-core for ground rules, exam matching, item writing and how the page looks: plain text by default, boxes only for the content guide-core section 7 lists. This file sets what the notes and practice contain. If the course also has real calculations (for example stoichiometry or dosage), load guide-procedural as the second subject skill for those parts.

## Why

- Labels placed on the diagram beat a separate legend (spatial contiguity, supported in 22 of 22 tests, median d about 1.10; Ginns 2006, d about 0.72).
- Cues that show the structure of the material help retention (g about 0.53) and transfer (g about 0.33) (Schneider et al. 2018).
- Breaking a complex process into segments helps (Rey et al. 2019, d about 0.36).
- "Just draw it" doesn't help. Drawing helps only when the learner explains while drawing or compares their drawing with a correct one (2025 meta-analysis: g about 0.35 with that support, no effect without it).
- Terms stick best through cued recall spread over time. Keyword mnemonics are low value (Dunlosky et al. 2013). Mixing helps for look-alike structures or processes, not for vocabulary lists (Brunmair and Richter 2019).

## Notes

- **Processes** (the main unit for anything that happens in order): a plain numbered list where each step says what happens, which structure does it, and why it leads to the next step, with a simple flow diagram (guide-core flow markup) right above it. Explanation around it is plain text.
- **KEY box** only for what the exam expects stated exactly: a formal definition the materials give, a named law or equation, or the official stages of a process the course names.
- **Labeled diagrams:** simple diagrams built in the page (labeled boxes and arrows, or a basic SVG) with labels on the diagram itself, never a separate legend. For complex anatomy figures that can't be rebuilt simply, point to the figure by its slide or figure number and describe the key parts in text.
- **Structure-function tables** when several structures are described on the same features: structure | where it is | what it does | what happens if it fails. Only the columns the materials support.
- **Terms** are defined in the text where they first appear. A terminology table only for a term-heavy topic where many terms share the same features (meaning, location, function).
- **Comparison tables** for look-alike structures or processes (for example two transport mechanisms or two similar reactions).

## Practice

All multiple choice and true/false practice goes in the week quiz (and the final quiz in a multi-week guide).
- **Cued recall:** term to function, structure to location, process step to what comes next.
- **Mechanism prediction:** "If this enzyme is blocked, what happens next?" or "What changes downstream if X rises?" Wrong options come from common misconceptions or the step that's out of order.
- **Sequence items:** put the steps of a process in order (orderings as options).
- **Diagram items:** a labeled diagram with letters; "which letter is the structure that does X?"
- **Draw and compare** (optional, a single line after the week quiz): draw the process or structure from memory, then compare with the guide's diagram and note what you missed.
- Put look-alike structures and processes in neighboring questions, not vocabulary lists.

## In a learning guide

- Each topic: the processes, diagrams and tables above, with plain-text explanation.
- No practice inside topics. The week quiz covers the week's terms, processes and predictions.
- Short-on-time mode: the processes and tables, one example each.

## In a study guide

- Core notes: terms, structures and process steps in cue-table form.
- Comparison tables: look-alike structures and processes.
- Methods: each key process once as a flow with its steps.
- Practice: week quizzes and the final quiz weighted toward prediction and diagram items, with free-recall "explain the process" prompts after the quiz if the exam has short answers.

## Avoid

- Boxing explanations or whole processes that the exam doesn't want word for word.
- Lists of terms with no function or location attached.
- Diagrams with a separate legend.
- Keyword mnemonics as the main memory tool.
- "Draw it" prompts with no comparison step.
